title: "Blind, Laser-Validated Environment Estimation for Crowdsourced GNSS Reference Stations"

abstract: |
    Community-driven GNSS reference networks built on low-cost dual-frequency receivers such as the u-blox ZED-F9P are rapidly expanding dense real-time-kinematic (RTK) coverage far beyond government continuously-operating reference stations. Centipede-RTK across Europe and Africa, the volunteer "Goodwill" (Zen'i) network in Japan, and emerging decentralized physical-infrastructure models now underpin precision agriculture, aerial delivery, and other autonomous applications. Their open participation model, however, introduces a quality-assurance gap that is absent in professional networks: stations are installed by non-experts on balconies, window ledges, and rooftops, without the site surveys, obstruction metadata, or antenna calibration that survey-grade networks rely on. Severe multipath and non-line-of-sight (NLOS) reception degrade not only the host station but, more consequentially, every rover using it as an RTK base, because base multipath is station-specific rather than common-mode and therefore does not cancel in the double difference. Across thousands of unsurveyed nodes, station-by-station inspection is infeasible, and a single silently degraded base corrupts the corrections delivered to all of its users.
    This paper presents a blind, map-free estimator that recovers a station's multipath environment from its own dual-frequency signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and code-minus-carrier observables alone, with no 3D model, no external database, and no site visit. The method repurposes GNSS Interferometric Reflectometry (GNSS-IR)—conventionally a geophysical sensing technique for snow depth, soil moisture, and water level—by inverting the direct-plus-reflected interference for the geometry of the obstructing environment rather than for a geophysical quantity. Two complementary observables are exploited: the SNR records the amplitude modulation from coherent superposition of direct and reflected signals, and the multipath linear combination (MP LC), formed as code-minus-carrier, isolates the geometric path delay. Each satellite arc is segmented at cycle slips, detrended by a second-order polynomial in sin(E) to preserve the low-frequency multipath oscillation, and analysed with a normalized Lomb-Scargle periodogram suited to irregular sampling. Ground reflectors are resolved in the sin(E) domain (height H = flambda/2) and vertical walls in the cos(E) domain (distance D = flambda/2), classified by which domain yields the larger spectral power. Reliability is enforced by three physical constraints: multi-frequency consensus (reflector distance is wavelength-independent, so L1 and L2 must agree within 0.2 m), dual-observable consensus (SNR and MP LC are independent estimators of the same geometry), and temporal persistence (a real fixed reflector reappears whenever a satellite traverses its direction). Surviving detections are spread over their first-Fresnel-zone footprints and fused into a 5-degree-by-5-degree azimuth-elevation grid of variance inflation factors V(a,e) in [1, V_cap], with V_cap = 3 throughout. A normalized periodogram makes the peak height independent of sample count, so the same thresholds hold for 1 Hz and 30 s stations without any correction factor; a site-level quality gate disables the map when the dual-observable validation rate falls below 10 percent, classifying the presence of a coherent specular reflector—a quantity orthogonal to teqc sky visibility.
    The estimator is validated directly against an independently laser-surveyed pilot station, F9P1, mounted on a residential balcony in Kamakura, Japan, whose surroundings were measured in situ with an optical distance meter to few-centimetre absolute accuracy, finer than public satellite imagery. With no prior knowledge of the site, the blind estimator recovers its asymmetric structure: 94 percent of consensus-validated wall detections fall in the obstructed north-west-through-east arc containing the apartment wall and metallic water heater, while fewer than 1.5 percent fall in the open south-west. Recovered distances match the survey—a near-field wall cluster at the 10th/25th percentiles of 1.01/1.42 m coincides with the water heater (0.89 m) and wall (1.39 m), and the direction-resolved horizontal-reflector median of 2.0 to 2.4 m matches the surveyed antenna height above ground (2.18 m). The gate autonomously enables a map at 10 of 21 stations and leaves it empty at the 11 reflector-poor sites, with no per-site tuning.
    The paper then asks systematically whether this per-direction map can improve positioning, across three operator families—direction-dependent variance inflation, satellite exclusion and partial ambiguity resolution, and empirical azimuth-elevation phase correction—in both single-point (SPP) and RTK modes, evaluated against per-station PPP-static truth (SPP) and GSI F5 daily coordinates of paired GEONET rovers (RTK). In single-point mode the prior is neutral: applying the VIF map changes H68 and V68 by less than one centimetre at every one of the 21 stations. This is not a weak operator but subsumption—an a-posteriori robust estimator (IGG-III residual re-weighting with a pre-robust gate) already down-weights the same bad-direction satellites the a-priori prior targets, and because every satellite enters the solution independently, the residual-based rejector subsumes the prior. The gate is thereby validated as do-no-harm: enabling VIF degrades no station, which in a volunteer network is itself valuable because a mistaken intervention at a healthy base lowers availability for every rover it serves.
    In RTK the prior has a genuine opening, because base multipath cannot be removed a-posteriori from a double difference. Yet at the only severely-obstructed base available—F9P1—it still loses. The stock legacy configuration is broken every day (median FIX H95 of 7.1 m, metre-scale FIX standard deviation, dominated by wrong-integer fixes). A tightened global double-difference innovation gate (innov5, a 5 m threshold matched to low-cost code multipath) rescues the site robustly across the week: median FIX H95 collapses by a factor of 28 to 0.25 m, the fix rate quadruples, gross wrong fixes fall from 11.6 percent to 0.6 percent, and correct usable fixes rise from 4.0 to 21.3 percent of all epochs. Crucially, innov5 does not consult the map. All three per-direction operators fail to beat it: the per-direction gate matches innov5 on five of seven days but underperforms on two, VIF-guided partial ambiguity resolution is decisively worse, and an empirical phase model trained on six days removes essentially nothing on the held-out day. The cause is physical and common to all three: with 69 percent of F9P1's active map cells at the cap, the site is obstructed in nearly every direction, leaving essentially no clean geometry to preserve, up-weight, or transfer a correction from, while its damaging errors are non-repeatable NLOS that only an epoch-wise gate catches as they occur. A network-scale do-no-harm test confirms the complementary case: across the ten-station Zeni network under stock legacy, the catastrophic wrong-fix regime is unique to F9P1, and every empty-map station delivers millimetre-to-centimetre FIX standard deviation with no metre-scale tail.
    These results yield a two-step, survey-free activation rule—a blind-environment classification step followed by a legacy RTK health check—that restricts intervention to environment-affected bases, and an accompanying failure-mode taxonomy that delineates where a per-direction prior does and does not help, identifying the partially-obstructed broken base (absent from the present data) as the open regime. Evaluated across 21 stations from three independent networks on three continents (Japan, Europe, Africa), spanning low-cost u-blox, Septentrio, and NovAtel hardware from open sky to a severely obstructed balcony, the work contributes a laser-validated blind site-characterization tool for crowdsourced networks and a clear delineation of where environment priors help positioning: in site quality control and activation, not in direction-resolved measurement weighting. The practical message is that blind environment estimation lowers the barrier to high-quality participation by supplying, without any site survey or calibration, both a physical site characterization and a reliable answer to which stations need attention—while the correction of their measurements is best left to a-posteriori estimators.
